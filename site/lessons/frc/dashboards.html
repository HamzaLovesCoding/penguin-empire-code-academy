Dashboards & AdvantageScope

1 Why this matters

The cutout nobody could see

A motor on the team's robot kept cutting out. Nobody could work out why, because every time somebody looked at the live number it was fine. The failure was over before anyone could turn their head.

Graphing amperage over time in AdvantageScope showed it immediately. The motor was hitting a current limit and dropping out, and the spike lasted a fraction of a second. A live value cannot show you something that happens for fifty milliseconds.

A second beat from the same team: they used SmartDashboard until it was on its way out, then moved to Glass. Your dashboard is a season decision, not a permanent one.

2 Three tools, three time horizons

Everything in this lesson comes down to one question: over what span of time do you need to see?

ToolShows youGood for
Driver Station consoleAn event that happened, as textErrors, exceptions, a message you printed
Live dashboardA named value, right nowIs the arm where I think it is? Is vision seeing a tag?
AdvantageScopeA value over time, including after the factAnything intermittent, and anything you cannot watch while it happens

Choosing well is most of your debugging speed. A problem that happens once in ten tries cannot be caught with a live number, because by the time you look at the screen the moment has gone.

3 How a value gets to your laptop

Your code runs on the roboRIO. Dashboards run on your laptop. The thing connecting them is NetworkTables, a shared table of named values that both sides can read.

Your code writes a value under a name. Every dashboard connected to the robot sees it appear. That is the whole mechanism, and it is why a dashboard value differs from a printed line:

println one line of text, once, gone into the scroll NetworkTables a NAMED value that keeps updating, so it can be graphed

The naming part is what makes graphing possible. A trace needs an identity that persists across fifty updates a second.

Verify for current season

This is the most season dependent page in the whole track. WPILib states that SmartDashboard and Shuffleboard "do not have a person to maintain them" and "are deprecated and will be removed for the 2027 season." That is exactly the change the team went through when they moved off SmartDashboard. Check what your team is using this year before you build tooling around one.

For the work in this course, the split is simple. Use a live dashboard while you are standing next to the robot and want to see a number. Use AdvantageScope when the interesting thing is over before you can look.

5 Reading a trace

A trace is a value plotted against time. Three shapes cover most of what you will see this season. In all three, the flat line is the output your code commanded and the curve is the velocity that resulted.

Trace A. Velocity rises and settles on the commanded level. This is what healthy looks like.

Trace B. Velocity overshoots and swings above and below the target before settling. You will meet this shape properly in Unit 6, where it means the control gains are too aggressive.

Trace C. Velocity holds, then falls off a cliff and comes back, while the commanded output never changes. Your code did not do this. Something on the hardware side cut the motor off.

Trace C is the team's war story drawn out. If you were watching a live number, you would see a healthy value both before and after the drop and nothing in between. The graph is the only place the failure exists.

6 Naming what you publish

Two habits worth forming before you publish anything.

Name for a stranger. Six weeks from now, at a competition, you will have three traces on screen. A value called value1 tells nobody anything, including you. A value called arm/position needs no explanation. The name is the only documentation a trace ever gets.

Publish deliberately. Twenty values updating fifty times a second costs loop time and buries the three you actually need. Publish what answers a question you have.

dashboard
A live view of the named values your code publishes.
NetworkTables
The shared table of named values that carries data between the robot and your laptop.
AdvantageScope
A tool that graphs those values over time and reads back log files after the fact.
trace
One value plotted against time. The shape of it is the information.

8 Common mistakes

Watch out for these
  • Confusing a dashboard with the Driver Station. The Driver Station enables the robot. A dashboard displays values. Different window, different job.
  • Treating println and a dashboard as the same thing. Printing sends a one off line of text. A dashboard shows a named value that keeps updating, which is what makes it graphable.
  • Thinking AdvantageScope is for advanced teams. It is the fastest way to find an intermittent problem, and reading a trace is easier than reading a log.
  • Publishing everything so you do not miss anything. Twenty values at fifty times a second costs loop time and buries the three that matter.
  • Assuming one dashboard is "the FRC dashboard." Glass is this team's current choice. SmartDashboard was, until it was not.
