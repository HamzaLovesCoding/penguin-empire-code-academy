1 Why this matters

One broken wire kills the whole loop

The CAN bus is a loop. Every motor controller sits on the same pair of wires, and the chain runs out from the roboRIO, through each device, and back. One crimp that vibrated apart during a match is enough. Every device past the break stops answering.

This happens often. Expect it. It is the reason an experienced programmer, watching several motors go dead at once, does not start by re-reading their code.

2 The roboRIO

The roboRIO is the computer bolted to the robot. WPILib calls it "the main robot controller" and says it "serves as the brain for the robot running team-generated code." You write code on your laptop, build it, and deploy it onto the roboRIO. From then on the roboRIO runs it, on its own, about fifty times a second.

Two facts to keep:

  • Your code does not run on your laptop. The laptop sends commands and displays output. Everything else happens on the roboRIO.
  • You image it every season. Imaging flashes the roboRIO with the current year's system image using the roboRIO Imaging Tool, which arrives with the FRC Game Tools you install in Unit 2. An unimaged roboRIO will not accept this season's code.
Verify for current season

Which roboRIO your team runs matters. Team 2551 used a roboRIO 1 and planned to move to a roboRIO 2, which boots from a microSD card and is imaged differently. Check the current WPILib and NI documentation for your model before imaging anything.

3 The radio

The radio is the robot's wireless access point. It connects to the roboRIO over Ethernet and gives your laptop and the Driver Station a way to reach the robot without a cable.

The current FRC radio is the Vivid-Hosting VH-109. WPILib notes it "uses Wi-Fi 6E to avoid the common congestion problems that plague 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi networks," and it carries four Ethernet ports, so most robots no longer need a separate network switch. You configure it by connecting over Ethernet and opening http://radio.local/, where you enter your team number.

Three behaviours will cost you time if you do not know them:

  • The radio takes one to two minutes to come up after the robot is powered on. New members read the lack of a connection as a broken robot and start changing things. Wait first.
  • At competition you do not configure it yourself. WPILib is explicit: "At competition, configuration will be done by a provided computer and manual configuration using this page should not be used." You take the robot to the field's setup station and their computer handles it. Afterwards you reconfigure it for your own shop network.
  • You can bypass it entirely. Plug an Ethernet cable from your laptop straight into the roboRIO. That is called tethering, and it removes the radio from the problem. It is the fastest way to find out whether a connection failure is a radio problem or a robot problem.
The single most useful move in Unit 1

When you cannot connect, tether by Ethernet before you change anything. If the tether works, the robot and your code are fine and the radio is the suspect. If the tether also fails, stop blaming Wi-Fi.

4 The CAN bus

CAN is the wiring that carries instructions from the roboRIO to every motor controller. It is a green and yellow twisted pair, and the important part is its shape.

CAN is one shared loop, not one cable per motor. The pair leaves the roboRIO, passes through every device in turn, goes through the Power Distribution Hub, and returns to the roboRIO. Because all devices share the same two wires, each device needs a unique number so the roboRIO can address exactly one of them. That number is its CAN ID, and it gets a full lesson in Unit 2.

Under the hood the roboRIO sends a heartbeat message on the bus every 20 ms. Devices that stop hearing it, or that the roboRIO stops hearing from, report a timeout. That is the origin of the error message you will see more than any other:

Driver Station console
ERROR  CAN ID 16 timed out

Read it as "device number 16 stopped answering." At this point in the course that is all you need. The full diagnosis lives in CAN IDs, and the competition version of this exact message shows up again in Unit 12.

5 The PDH

The Power Distribution Hub is where battery power gets split up and sent out to everything on the robot. REV's hub has 20 high current channels rated to 40 A, three low current channels at 15 A, and one switchable low current channel. Older robots use CTRE's Power Distribution Panel, which does the same job through auto resetting circuit breakers.

The part people forget: the PDH is also a device on the CAN bus. It reports channel currents and it sits on the loop like any motor controller does. That is why it holds a reserved CAN ID on most teams, and why a problem at the PDH can break communication and not just power.

7 What breaks and what you see

This table is the payoff of the whole lesson. Read it as symptom to cause, not cause to symptom, because symptoms are what you actually get handed.

What is brokenWhat you see
RoboRIO not imaged for this seasonCode will not deploy, or deploys and will not run
Radio still bootingNo Communications light. Wait one to two minutes
Radio not configured for the fieldCannot connect at competition, works fine in the shop
One CAN wire brokenDevices past the break time out with CAN ID n timed out
Wrong CAN ID in your codeThat one motor does nothing. Everything else is fine
Motor unplugged from its controllerThe controller answers normally and nothing spins

The reasoning skill underneath the table is narrowing by scope:

  • Everything dead. Look upstream at power, the roboRIO, or the very start of the bus.
  • One branch dead. Look for a break in the CAN loop between the last device that works and the first that does not.
  • One device dead. Look at that device's ID and its own wiring.

9 Common mistakes

Watch out for these
  • Thinking CAN works like USB. There is no cable per motor. It is one shared loop, which is exactly why every device needs a unique ID and why one break takes out everything after it.
  • Reading "no communication" as "my code is broken." Your code has not been reached yet. Communications is the network layer. Robot Code is a separate indicator on the Driver Station.
  • Treating imaging as one time setup. The roboRIO gets imaged every season, because every season ships a new WPILib and a new image.
  • Blaming code for a mechanical break. If the console is clean and the encoder reports movement but nothing happens, the failure is past the motor. Check the belt.
